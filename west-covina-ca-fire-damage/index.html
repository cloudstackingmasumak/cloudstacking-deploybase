West Covina property owners need to secure a fire damaged home quickly to prevent weather intrusion, theft, and further loss.

In the hours right after firefighters leave, a fire damaged home is often left with broken windows, damaged doors, and sometimes an opening in the roof or exterior wall. This creates an immediate vulnerability that has nothing to do with the fire itself. An unsecured property invites weather damage, pests, and unfortunately in some cases theft or vandalism, all of which can compound the losses a West Covina family is already dealing with.

Board up services address this gap, using plywood, tarps, and temporary fencing to seal off a damaged structure until permanent repairs can begin. This is often one of the first calls a homeowner makes after a fire, sometimes even before the insurance claim is fully underway, because the risk of additional damage grows with every hour a property sits exposed.

This article covers why securing a fire damaged property matters so much, what the board up process actually involves, and what West Covina homeowners need to know about timing, materials, and how this early step connects to the insurance claim that follows. Getting this step right from the start protects everything else the restoration process is working to save.

Why Immediate Securing Matters So Much

The period right after a fire is uniquely vulnerable for a property. Broken windows and damaged doors leave a home open to the elements, and in West Covina's dry climate, wind driven dust and debris can enter quickly, compounding cleanup work that already needs to happen from the fire itself. Rain, though less frequent, can cause significant additional damage to a structure that has already lost some of its protective barriers.

Beyond weather, an unsecured property signals vulnerability to anyone passing by. Vacant looking homes with visible damage sometimes attract trespassers, whether curious onlookers or people looking to take advantage of the situation. Copper pipes, appliances, and any remaining valuables inside an unsecured fire damaged home represent real risk if the property sits open for even a day or two.

There is also a liability angle that many West Covina homeowners do not think about immediately. An unsecured property with structural hazards, exposed wiring, or unstable flooring creates real danger for anyone who might wander in, including neighborhood children or curious passersby. Securing the property quickly reduces this risk considerably and is often a requirement under most homeowner insurance policies as a condition of continued coverage on the loss.

What a Professional Board Up Actually Involves

A proper board up goes beyond just nailing a sheet of plywood over a broken window. Professional crews assess every point of entry into the damaged structure, including windows, doors, and any openings created by the fire itself or by firefighting efforts like ventilation holes cut into a roof to release smoke and heat during the response.

Plywood is typically cut to size for each specific opening and secured using methods that resist forced entry, not just casual movement. This often includes security screws or specialized mounting hardware rather than standard nails, which can be pried loose relatively easily. For larger openings, like a collapsed wall section, temporary framing may be needed to support the board up material properly.

In West Covina, crews familiar with local building styles know that many homes use stucco exteriors, which requires different mounting techniques than a home with wood or vinyl siding to avoid causing additional damage to the exterior finish during installation. Roof damage often needs tarping as a separate step from window and door board up, using heavy duty tarps secured with battens to prevent wind from lifting the material and exposing the structure again during the wait for permanent repairs.

Roof Tarping After Fire and Ventilation Damage

Roof damage is common after house fires, sometimes from the flames themselves burning through roofing materials, and sometimes from firefighters cutting ventilation holes as part of standard suppression tactics. Either way, an open roof exposes the entire structure below to weather, making tarping a priority alongside window and door board up.

Proper roof tarping uses heavy duty, UV resistant tarps secured with wooden battens or specialized fasteners designed to withstand wind without tearing the material or pulling free from the roof deck. A poorly secured tarp can actually cause more damage than no tarp at all if it flaps loose in the wind and further damages already weakened roofing materials.

West Covina's generally dry climate means roof tarps often need to withstand more wind exposure than heavy rain, but seasonal weather patterns can bring occasional storms that make a properly secured tarp essential rather than optional. Professional crews check tarps periodically while a property sits in this temporary state, since materials can shift over time from wind, temperature changes, and the general settling that happens on a fire damaged roof structure awaiting permanent repair.

Materials and Methods That Actually Hold Up

Not all board up materials perform equally, and choosing the right approach affects how well a property stays protected during what can be a lengthy repair timeline. Exterior grade plywood, typically at least half inch thick, holds up far better than thinner material against both weather and any attempted forced entry compared to lighter options.

Fasteners matter as much as the plywood itself. Security screws, rather than standard nails, resist prying and provide a more durable hold over the weeks a board up might need to remain in place. Edges and corners, often the weakest points in any board up installation, need particular attention since these are the areas most likely to fail first under stress from wind or attempted entry.

For West Covina properties with larger openings or significant structural damage, temporary fencing sometimes supplements board up materials, creating an additional barrier around the property perimeter. This is particularly common for homes with severe damage where the structure itself may not be fully secure even with plywood in place, adding a layer of protection while the insurance and repair process moves forward over the following weeks.

Insurance Requirements and Documentation

Most homeowner insurance policies include language requiring the policyholder to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage after a covered loss like a fire. Board up and securing services generally fall directly under this requirement, and failing to secure a property adequately can, in some cases, affect coverage for any additional damage that occurs afterward. Reviewing this section of the policy early avoids surprises later in the claims process.

Keeping receipts and documentation for board up services is important, since these costs are typically reimbursable under most policies as emergency mitigation expenses. Photographing the property both before and after the board up work provides useful documentation for the broader claim, showing the insurer that reasonable protective measures were taken promptly after the loss occurred.

West Covina homeowners working with a restoration company that also handles board up services often find the documentation process smoother, since these companies are familiar with what insurers expect to see and can provide detailed invoices and photos as part of a coordinated claim package. This connects the emergency securing work directly to the larger restoration timeline rather than treating it as a separate, disconnected expense.

Choosing a Board Up and Securing Provider

Not every contractor who claims to offer board up services actually specializes in fire damaged properties, and the difference shows up in the quality and durability of the work. A general handyman might board a window competently, but a crew experienced specifically in fire and disaster response understands the fuller picture, including how to handle structurally compromised openings, smoke damaged framing near a boarded area, and the documentation insurers expect to see.

Response time matters more than almost anything else when selecting a provider. A property left exposed overnight, or even for several hours during a hot, windy West Covina afternoon, carries real risk, so a company that can dispatch a crew quickly after the first call has real value beyond simple convenience. Many restoration companies advertise emergency board up as a first response service specifically because of this urgency.

It also helps to choose a provider who can carry the relationship forward into the rest of the restoration process, from structural drying through contents cleaning and final repairs. West Covina homeowners who select a single company for board up and the broader restoration work often find communication easier, since the same team already has photos, an initial damage assessment, and a working relationship with the insurance adjuster established from that very first emergency visit to the property.

How Long a Property Typically Stays Boarded Up

The length of time a fire damaged home remains boarded up varies considerably based on the extent of damage and how quickly the insurance and repair process moves. Minor damage affecting a single room might only require board up for a few weeks while repairs are scheduled and completed. More extensive structural damage can mean months of boarded windows and doors while reconstruction takes place.

During this period, periodic checks on the board up materials matter, since West Covina's weather, while generally mild, can still stress materials over an extended timeframe through temperature swings and occasional wind events. A board up that looked secure on day one can loosen over months without maintenance, particularly around fasteners and edges that take the most stress.

Homeowners should stay in contact with whoever installed the board up if the timeline stretches longer than initially expected, since materials may need reinforcement or replacement for extended coverage. A boarded up home is meant as a temporary bridge to full repairs, not a permanent solution, and keeping that protection intact throughout the entire wait protects everything else that the rest of the restoration process is working to save inside the West Covina property.
