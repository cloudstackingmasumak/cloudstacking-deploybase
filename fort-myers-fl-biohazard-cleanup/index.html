The Role of Personal Protective Equipment

Professional crime scene cleanup requires extensive personal protective equipment to protect workers from pathogen exposure. Full-body suits, respirators, multiple layers of gloves, and shoe covers are standard requirements. Professionals also wear eye protection and head covering to prevent any contact with contaminated materials. The level of PPE required varies depending on the specific hazards present at the scene. Blood contamination requires different protection than fecal matter or other biological materials. Professional teams select appropriate PPE based on their assessment of the specific contamination present. Workers receive training in proper donning and doffing procedures to prevent contamination during gear changes. Most people attempting DIY cleanup have no PPE at all and expose themselves to extreme risks. Even with basic gloves and masks, protection is inadequate for bloodborne pathogen exposure. Professional teams invest in quality equipment and training to keep themselves safe while protecting your property.

Removal of Contaminated Materials

Materials saturated or heavily contaminated with blood or bodily fluids in an Fort Myers crime scene cannot be adequately disinfected and must be removed. Carpet and padding that have absorbed blood through to the backing must go. Flooring damaged by contamination or water damage from cleanup efforts requires removal. Drywall exposed to significant contamination often cannot be safely restored and requires replacement. Furniture, mattresses, bedding, or other items heavily contaminated cannot be salvaged. Professional crews carefully removes these materials, placing them in medical waste containers rated for biohazard transport and disposal. All contaminated materials go to licensed medical waste disposal facilities, not regular trash. Professionals provide disposal documentation confirming proper handling.
