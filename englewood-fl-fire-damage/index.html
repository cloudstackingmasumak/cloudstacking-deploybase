How Wildfire Smoke Enters Englewood Homes

Wildfire smoke is not just visible haze from distant fires. It contains microscopic particles of combusted vegetation, chemicals, and ash that behave like soot. In Englewood, these particles enter homes through the path of least resistance. Cracks around doors and windows are primary entry points. Gaps around electrical outlets, baseboards, and wall penetrations also allow smoke inside. If doors are left open during wildfire events, massive amounts of smoke enter quickly.

Ventilation systems in Englewood homes also draw in wildfire smoke. Bathroom exhaust fans, kitchen range hoods, and clothes dryer vents all pull outside air through ductwork and sometimes indoor spaces. Once a small amount of wildfire smoke enters through ventilation, it spreads throughout the entire home's air volume. HVAC systems that operate during smoky conditions pull outdoor air through return ducts, distributing smoke-filled air to every room in your Englewood house.

In some Englewood homes, attics and crawl spaces fill with smoke before it descends into living areas. Once smoke reaches insulation and wooden framing in the attic, it clings to surfaces and becomes difficult to remove without aggressive remediation. The deeper the smoke penetrates into your Englewood home's cavities, the longer it takes to eliminate the odor and contamination.
