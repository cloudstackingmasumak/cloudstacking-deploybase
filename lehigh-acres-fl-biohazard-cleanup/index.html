Bloodborne Pathogens Standard

OSHA's Bloodborne Pathogens Standard establishes mandatory requirements for anyone handling potentially infectious materials. The standard requires employers to develop exposure control plans, provide training to employees, and maintain documentation. Teams must identify which employees have potential exposure and implement protection measures. The standard mandates use of universal precautions, treating all blood and bodily fluids as potentially infectious. Personal protective equipment becomes mandatory, not optional, for anyone with potential exposure. Our teams follow these requirements absolutely, not because someone's watching, but because they're essential for safety. Employees receive regular training on bloodborne pathogen exposure and protection. Compliance with the Bloodborne Pathogens Standard is ongoing, documented, and verified through inspection and records. These standards exist because workers died or got seriously ill before they were enacted, making them lessons learned through tragedy. The Bloodborne Pathogens Standard protects our workers and everyone they come into contact with, making it one of the most important regulatory frameworks in the industry. Biohazard Waste Disposal Regulations

Federal regulations govern how biohazard waste is collected, transported, and disposed. Medical waste cannot go into regular trash, it must go through certified medical waste disposal facilities. Containers must be properly labeled and marked to indicate biohazard contents. Transportation must follow strict procedures to prevent spills or exposure. Documentation must verify that waste reached an appropriate disposal facility. Our teams coordinate with certified medical waste contractors who handle all transport and disposal. This regulatory framework ensures biohazard materials don't end up in landfills or compromise environmental safety.
